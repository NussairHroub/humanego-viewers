One demonstration of cup → plate, with object points re-extracted from every frame's SAM mask and lifted to metric 3D with MoGe-2 depth, anchored per frame on MPS semidense points. The contrast with the previous representation is the point of this page: the washed-out red points are the legacy static pool, triangulated once from the approach window and frozen for the whole episode — it stays at the pickup location while the real cup is carried away. Click any point to read its world xyz. The hand channel switch cycles the three representations the extractor can emit — they are the literal hand_mode settings, each read from its own run of the stage, so what you see is the artifact rather than a re-derivation.

Coordinates are centimetres in the recording's MPS closed-loop world frame — the same frame the policy's reference transform is built from. Object points are the 20 equidistant inner-contour samples of that frame's mask (the identical sampler that produced the original keypoints), lifted through a per-frame metric depth scale. They are re-sampled independently every frame, so a given index is not a tracked correspondence across time — only the set is meaningful. Frames whose mask fails the quality gate hold the last good points rather than snapping back to the static pool; those show as held in the inspector. Against independent MPS semidense points this lift is closer to truth than the legacy triangulated pool on both objects (cup +14.3 mm vs +17.7 mm, plate −22.7 mm vs +24.6 mm). The three hand channels are the extractor's hand_mode options, each rendered from its own run over this recording; the object channel is bit-identical across all three (max |diff| 0.000000000), so switching the hand representation cannot disturb the objects.
