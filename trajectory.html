The 6-DoF closed-loop trajectory Project Aria MPS solves for every recording — the path the headset actually travelled, at 1 kHz, in the same metric world frame everything else on this site is expressed in. The blue line is the device path over the whole recording; the tan frustums are the rectified RGB camera poses (c2w) at the frames the pipeline consumes, which is the pose the policy's reference transform is built from. Click any pose for its position and orientation. The frustums sit in one tight cluster on purpose: the pipeline only extracts the manipulation window, which spans a median 62% of each recording in time but about 5% of its spatial extent — the wearer walks to the table first, then stands still and works. Use the camera frustums switch to show them all, reveal them progressively along the timeline, or follow just the live one. Every recording is in the grid below — click a tile to load it here.

Poses come from slam/closed_loop_trajectory.csv, the MPS closed-loop solution: globally optimised against loop closures, so it is metric and drift-corrected, unlike the open-loop trajectory shipped beside it. Positions are centimetres in the recording's world frame; orientation is RPY (ZYX, degrees). The path is the device frame sampled from ~25,000 poses per recording; the frustums are the rectified RGB camera pose at pipeline frames. MPS also reports a per-pose quality score and the gravity direction, both shown here. Worth noting: this trajectory is what defines the world frame for the whole dataset, but the semidense point cloud MPS produces alongside it is downloaded, validated and then never read by the released pipeline.
