Two closed-loop runs on the ALOHA rig, 31 August 2026, driven by a policy that never sees pixels. The checkpoint is the HumanEgo recipe trained with img_name: None (runs/cup_plate_02/001_HumanEgo_baseline_NoRGB) — its vision branch only ever saw all-zero images, so the only thing carrying information into the network is the Interaction-Centric Token matrix: hand pose, object poses, grasp state. Both runs complete the cup → plate transfer. Each tile starts on the plain camera view; switch to all panels for the detections and the gripper overlay. Playback is one frame per policy step (~25× real time) — the header carries true elapsed seconds.

Why the “policy input” panel is empty. When the checkpoint was trained on zero images, feeding it a real inpainted frame at deploy would be an out-of-distribution input that fails silently — so ICTPolicy sets zero_image, the loop skips LaMa inpainting and the gripper render entirely, and no clean image is ever produced. Strictly this is not obs_mode="ict_only": the visual tokens still exist and context-norm still sees them, they just carry zeros. Same observation at deploy, different architecture on paper.

Neither run ended on the policy's own judgement. The termination head peaked at 0.13 against a 0.80 threshold and was still climbing when the operator stopped each episode — so “success” here means the transfer was executed, not that the policy knew it was finished. The obs_mode="ict_only" checkpoint tested on 25 August shows the opposite failure of the same head: on this rig it fired 0.96 at the very first step, before the gripper had closed, and stopped the episode after one decision. Pose-only tokens leave that head with no reliable signal in either direction.
